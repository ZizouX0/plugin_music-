The Golden Order

  1. Foundation — organize, gain stage, lock kick & bass, loudness stress-test
  2. Balance — level the hook, balance everything around it, reference
  3. Shape — EQ → dynamics → saturation, one problem at a time
  4. Space — reverb, delay, width
  5. Story — automation, transitions, energy arc
  6. Master — glue, loudness, true-peak, reference, bounce

Work top-down. Your “lead vocal” = kick + sub + main hook.

Gain Staging / Headroom

Plugins expect avg−18 dBFS
Channels peak−6 dBFS
Buses peak−3 dBFS
Master pre-limiterleave −6 dBFS
Rough mix under−5 dBFS

Kick = the loudest single element. Get loud at the master, not now.

Kick & Bass — The Engine

60% of the mix lives below 250 Hz. They must interlock, never collide.

Kick punch50–100 Hz + click 2–5 kHz
Sub/bassoff the kick’s fundamental; carve a dip
HP everything else20–35 Hz
Boomy kick? cut150–250 Hz

Coexist via EQ carving + sidechain ducking. Keep all sub-bass MONO.

EQ & Compression

4 jobs of EQ

  • Clean: boost Q≈1.5, sweep, find ugly, cut
  • Enhance: wide Q 0.3–2, ~3 dB
  • Transform: filter sweep = the build sound
  • Make room: dip where another lives

Mix-bus glue

Ratio 3:1 · attack ~100 ms · release ~5 ms · final 1–2 dB GR · breathes in time.

Saturation & Clipping

  • Compress — peaks down smoothly
  • Limit — hard wall (master)
  • Clip — chop peak tips, keep punch & loudness — great on kicks

Clip the kick · saturate the sub (survives phones) · saturate leads to cut.

Saturation = harmonics → warmth + translation on small speakers.

Master Chain & Delivery

  1. Subtractive/tonal EQ — broad balance, small moves
  2. Glue comp — 1–2 dB GR, slow
  3. Saturation / exciter — harmonics & cohesion
  4. Multiband (optional) — tame sub or harsh hi-mids
  5. True-peak limiter — push to target

Bounce the mix at −6 dBFS, limiter OFF. Export master: 24-bit WAV (+ MP3 320 for sharing). Check in mono, on phone, in the car, on earbuds.

Pro Techniques — Deeply Sourced

Settings used by manufacturers, mastering engineers, and audio-education sources. Tags: [MFR/ENG] = manufacturer- or engineer-stated · [RoT] = widely-cited rule of thumb. Full source URLs at the bottom — every number is verifiable.

Streaming Loudness Normalization (reference levels)

PlatformReferenceNotes & source
Spotify−14 LUFSOfficial. True-peak ≤ −1 dBTP; ≤ −2 dBTP if your master is louder than −14. [MFR]
Apple Music−16 LUFSSound Check. Industry-confirmed, not a first-party doc. [RoT]
YouTube−14 LUFSTurns loud tracks down only. [RoT]
Tidal / Amazon−14 LUFSTrack + album normalization. [RoT]
EBU R128 (broadcast)−23 LUFSMax −1 dBTP; ±0.5 LU tolerance. [MFR]
AES TD1008 (streaming rec.)−16 LUFSRecommended distribution target for music. [MFR]

Principle (Ian Shepherd): “Integrated LUFS is not a target… it should be the result, not the goal.” Master for the sound; let normalization turn it down. A −6 LUFS club master simply gets turned down ~8 dB on Spotify — your dynamics/limiting choices remain.

Mix-Bus “Glue” Compression

Ratio1.5:1–2:1 (up to 4:1) [MFR]
Attackslow ~30–50 ms (keep transients) [MFR]
Release~250 ms or Auto [MFR]
Gain reduction1–3 dB (“even 1 dB matters”) [MFR]

SSL preset: 2:1 · 30 ms · Auto release · 2–4 dB GR (Waves). NI Glue uses a faster 10 ms attack (cap ~15–20 ms). EDM tolerates 4–5 dB GR for “pump” (Attack Mag).

Drum Bus & Parallel Comp

Drum glue (SSL)4:1 · 10 ms · 0.1–0.3 rel · 4–6 dB GR [MFR]
EDM drum busattack ~3 ms · 2–10 dB GR [RoT]
Parallel “smash”fastest attack · ~10:1 · ~20 dB GR, blend under dry [MFR]

Faster attack = more clamp; ~10 ms preserves snare/kick punch.

Sidechain (measured settings)

Attack1–10 ms (~4 ms avoids click) [RoT]
Ratio5:1 kick→bass · 4:1 kick→synth · 8–20:1 for obvious pump [RoT]
Release~60 ms or timed to tempo [RoT]

Release-to-tempo: 60000 ÷ BPM = quarter-note ms (124 BPM ≈ 484 ms). Set release to recover just before the next kick.

Mastering Chain — Order & Settings

Signal order (iZotope “ideal chain”): corrective EQ → corrective multiband/dynamics (fast) → glue comp (low ratio, medium attack/release) → saturation → tonal “sweetening” EQ → stereo imaging → limiter (last). Do as little as needed. [MFR]

Master EQmoves < 3 dB, wide Q; HP rumble at 20–40 Hz (wide bell) [MFR]
Master comp1.25:1–2:1, 1–2 dB GR, slow-ish attack / fast release [RoT]
LimiterTrue-Peak ON · ceiling −1.0 dBTP · 4×/8× oversampling + ≥0.1 ms lookahead [MFR]

Ian Shepherd: “A few dB less peak limiting almost always sounds just as good, if not better.” Don’t over-limit. EDM: hard-clip transients before the limiter so kicks keep punch and the limiter works less (Sage Audio).

Pre-Master Bounce

  • Leave ~6 dB headroom — peaks ≤ −6 dBFS; “just don’t clip it.” [MFR]
  • 32-bit float won’t clip, but analog-emulation plugins still distort hot — keep channel levels lower throughout, not just the master.
  • Mix landing zone ~−25 to −19 LUFS integrated. [RoT]

Source: Ian Shepherd / Production Advice.

Stereo Width (sourced)

  • Keep lows mono below ~100–200 Hz. [RoT]
  • Widen highs (cymbals/hats/perc); pan synths. [MFR]
  • Haas: delay one side 5–35 ms (e.g. L 1 ms / R 25 ms, 100% wet) — never on sub <~200 Hz; can vanish in mono. [MFR]
  • Mid/Side is more mono-safe than Haas. Always check in mono.

Sources: Mastering The Mix, Audient, Sound on Sound.

⚠ Flags (sources disagree)

  • Mono freq: documented 100 Hz / 150 Hz; watch ≤200 Hz (vinyl); never crossover <75 Hz. 120 Hz is lore.
  • Apple −16 LUFS, YouTube/Tidal/Amazon −14: industry-confirmed, not first-party docs (Spotify & EBU are first-party).
  • Loudness philosophy split: EDM educators cite −6 to −7 LUFS; Shepherd argues against chasing LUFS / heavy limiting.
  • Mix-bus attack: slow ~30–50 ms (SoS/SSL) vs faster 10 ms (NI). All agree: low ratio, 1–4 dB GR.
